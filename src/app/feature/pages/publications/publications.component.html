M. Ravangard, M. Kishani Farahani, A. Rajabi-Ghahnavieh
Application of Big Data for Supermarket Refrigeration Systems with Development of Data-Driven Machine Learning Models
2025 15th Smart Grid Conference (SGC), Shiraz, Iran, 2–3 December 2025.
DOI: 10.1109/SGC69320.2025.11372268
Abstract

High-energy consumption is a hallmark of supermarket refrigeration, where these units often represent approximately 50% of the total electrical load. When operational faults occur, they trigger inflated power usage, higher maintenance expenditures, and a larger environmental footprint. This research introduces a machine learning (ML)-based framework for fault detection and diagnosis (FDD), specifically targeting the classification of six common malfunctions in CO2 refrigeration systems (CO2-RS). By utilizing a laboratory-scale CO2-RS for data acquisition, several tree-based models were trained and optimized via Grid Search Cross-Validation. The experimental results reveal that all evaluated architectures exceeded 99% classification accuracy, with the LightGBM model reaching a peak F1-score of 99.73%. These results highlight the efficacy of ML-integrated FDD systems in improving refrigeration performance, lowering operational overhead, and mitigating ecological impacts.

S. Akbari, M. M. Forootan, M. Ravangard, M. A. Bijarchi
Techno-Economic Assessment of Solar-Based Cu-Cl Thermochemical Hydrogen Production Plant: A Case Study
2025 10th International Conference on Technology and Energy Management (ICTEM), Tabriz, Iran, 21–23 April 2025.
DOI: 10.1109/ICTEM66196.2025.11063499
Abstract

As the global energy sector undergoes a transition towards decarbonization, Power-to-X technologies play a pivotal role in converting renewable electricity into usable fuels and chemicals. This study examines the technical and economic feasibility of a solar-powered hydrogen production system that is founded on the copper-chlorine (Cu–Cl) thermochemical cycle. A novel configuration is proposed, utilizing carbon dioxide as a heat transfer fluid (HTF) and integrating a heat recovery network to enhance efficiency. In contrast to numerous prior studies that depend on static models, this research employs dynamic, hourly simulations over an entire year, grounded in actual solar data, to evaluate performance under realistic conditions. The results indicate that this system is capable of achieving lower hydrogen production costs in comparison to traditional electrolysis, primarily due to a reduction in electricity consumption. Furthermore, any surplus electricity generated may be sold to the grid, thereby enhancing economic returns. Through multiobjective optimization, the optimal direct normal irradiance (DNI) for the solar input is identified as 881 W/m2, resulting in a hydrogen cost of about $5.5 per kilogram.
